## Findings

**F1 — P0 — `orderPayableBase` takes `max(invoiced, live lines)`.** `apps/api/src/modules/orders/orders.service.ts:2434-2456` (Appendix C). PRE-EXISTING design, now fed by live divergent data. The payment cap and every aging/collections reader currently resolve order 20260902-000108 to 7,079.24 while the customer holds an enforceable document for 6,880.45. This is actively misstating receivables today, and `max()` is unprincipled by construction: it inflates the ask when lines go up and quietly keeps the invoice figure when they go down. Customer-visible the moment an agent or the cap acts on it.

**F2 — P0 — issuance freezes an unvalidated, unserialized order state.** `apps/api/src/modules/orders/invoice.service.ts:652-668` (Appendix D): `computeInvoiceAmount` and the insert are not inside an order-row lock, there is no priced-line completeness check, and no confirmation that the figure about to be frozen is the figure the trader believes they're freezing. PRE-EXISTING guard absence; Case B (`INV-2026-0006`, 40x, already emailed to Dan Bunkering) is a NEW incident produced by it.

**F3 — P1 — priced fields and priced-line create/delete remain writable after issuance.** Order/order_items write paths; no counterpart to `savePaymentSchedule`'s refusal (Appendix G precedent). PRE-EXISTING capability; the A and C divergences are NEW incidents manufactured here. Root cause of F1's bad input and of every future divergence until blocked. Note Case B's post-issuance act was a line **create**, so any block must cover insert/delete, not just updates.

**F4 — P2 — the first-render guard is unreachable as a detection control.** `apps/api/src/modules/documents/document.service.ts:1455-1495` with the frozen early-return, and the order-scoped lookup at `:506-530` (Appendices A, B). PRE-EXISTING. Functionally harmless — the frozen artifact is correct to serve — but it is cited in the system description as the thing that "catches" drift, and it structurally cannot fire once a revision exists. It should stop being treated as a control either way: extended to detect drift, or explicitly demoted to a first-render self-consistency check.

**F5 — P2 — due-date anchor uses `deliveredAt ?? eta`, and `deliveredAt` is writable before DELIVERED.** `apps/api/src/modules/orders/invoice-amounts.ts:25-48`, issuance call site at `invoice.service.ts:652-668`, and the same pattern in `payment-schedule.service.ts:285-315` (`computeTrancheDueDate`, where the docs explicitly label it *provisional*). PRE-EXISTING; Case C incident NEW. Case C's frozen invoice is correct and needs no correction; the hazard is an issuance freezing CREDIT terms off a pre-delivery guess, and post-issuance `deliveredAt` edits making order-derived dates contradict the frozen `invoices.due_date`.

**F6 — P2 — no reconciliation exists anywhere.** The blast-radius table was produced by a manual audit, not by the system. NEW (missing control). Cheap to build, and it's the rollout evidence for everything above.

## Answers

**1. Interventions and order; is B separate?** Yes, B is a separate defect — different site (the freeze moment, not the writes that cross it afterward), different guard (issuance validation/transactionality), different precedent (Appendix G covers schedule edits; nothing validates what issuance freezes). Fixing F3 alone still lets garbage freeze; fixing F2 alone still lets A/C drift. They are one principle applied on both sides of the same boundary: **issuance is a hard freeze; nothing crosses it afterward, and nothing crosses it unvalidated at the moment.** Merge interventions 1 and 2: implement 2 as a server-side refusal whose error payload carries the frozen-vs-live delta and the void/reissue pointer — the UI renders exactly the "warning" of 1, but the edit does not go through. Warn-only leaves the defect live.

**2. Is freeze-on-issue still right?** Yes, unambiguously. The invoice is a document the customer holds; its number is never reused; void+reissue is the sanctioned correction path that preserves both artifacts for audit. Live-wins would mean the PDF in the customer's inbox is silently superseded by a figure they never agreed to — it breaks their accounting, your audit trail, the verify/QR trust story, and contradicts your own `savePaymentSchedule` precedent. The model is not the bug; the missing enforcement around it is.

**3. Collections: frozen invoice.** The receivable is the invoiced amount — you cannot send a dunning letter for 7,079.24 against an invoice stating 6,880.45. What breaks if live (or `max()`) wins: dunning letters and the payment cap, the payment-claim allocation (`claimUnallocatedPayments` re-points payments at invoice ids whose amounts no longer match what you're chasing), disputes, and audit. Make `orderPayableBase` invoice-authoritative with precise edge semantics: no live invoices → computed live lines (pre-issuance prospective figure); fully invoiced → sum of live non-VOID invoices; partially invoiced schedules → live invoices plus the computed share of unissued tranches (the mixed-currency fallback already returns `invoiced` when > 0, which is consistent). Keep the live figure visible as an *advisory* "unbilled delta" whose only action is void & reissue (or a supplementary invoice with a fresh number). What breaks when you flip: a wrong freeze like Case B gets *locked into* the money path until voided — that is a feature (the wrongness gets loud and forces the sanctioned correction), but it makes the ops remediation below mandatory, not optional.

**4. `deliveredAt ?? eta` as the CREDIT anchor:** wrong for a frozen invoice when `deliveredAt` can be a pre-delivery guess. The `eta` fallback is legitimate for *provisional* preview dates — that's literally what `computeTrancheDueDate` documents — but a frozen invoice must anchor on an actual delivery. So yes: issuance of a CREDIT invoice requires `DELIVERED` status with a non-null `deliveredAt`, atomically (the transition and issuance in one transaction); `computeInvoiceDueDate`'s `?? eta` path becomes unreachable for issued documents and stays for previews. Post-issuance `deliveredAt` edits are priced-field edits and get blocked under F3; a genuinely wrong due date goes through `voidOrderInvoice`'s existing `dueDate` override (Appendix F — the design for this already exists).

**5. Case B fix: both, layered.** A confirmation gate alone doesn't stop a concurrent-write race; a lock alone doesn't stop a trader issuing a half-built order at 08:32 and finishing the priced line at 08:37. Implement: (a) issuance inside a transaction holding a row lock on the order, with the amount **recomputed inside the lock** so any priced write serializes against the freeze; (b) a hard precondition — at least one complete priced line, status DELIVERED; (c) a confirmation payload carrying the figure (or order version) the UI showed the trader, re-checked inside the lock — an optimistic-concurrency token on the exact number being frozen; (d) the customer email fires only after that transaction commits.

**6. Before-the-rest vs. can-wait.** Customer-visible money, fix first: Case B's invoice itself (ops: void & reissue `INV-2026-0006` with trader-confirmed figures and customer comms — today, no code needed); and F1's money reads (first code change). Systemic, second wave: F3 block + F2 issuance guard. Internal consistency, third: F4 reclassification, F5 anchor discipline (subsumed by the F2/F3 work), side-by-side display and the F6 reconciliation job. Legacy data: `INV-2026-0003` → void & reissue at 7,079.24 after trader sign-off (never auto-reissue a computed figure — Case B is what unexamined mid-edit state looks like); `INV-2026-0001` → no reissue, due date is correct.

## Recommended implementation order

0. **Ops, same day:** void & reissue `INV-2026-0006`; assess and remediate `INV-2026-0003`; leave `INV-2026-0001`.
1. **First code change (highest value):** invoice-authoritative `orderPayableBase` + audit of aging/collections readers. It's the only finding misstating money toward customers through system behavior *every day*, the diff is small and testable, and it must land before the edit block so no order's payable basis silently shifts when the freeze starts being enforced. Tests: no-invoice fallback, voided-only, fully invoiced, partial tranche schedule, mixed currency.
2. **Priced-field freeze (F3):** server-side refusal on priced fields, line insert/delete, `deliveredAt`, credit terms, and bank/invoicing-company inputs once any live invoice exists, error payload carrying frozen-vs-live and the void/reissue path.
3. **Issuance guard (F2+F5):** locked recompute, priced-line precondition, confirmed-figure token, DELIVERED-with-`deliveredAt` requirement. Ship with step 2 or immediately before it; the recovery path (void/reissue) already exists, so no harmful window either way.
4. **Visibility and detection (F4/F6):** nightly frozen-vs-live reconciliation across all four instances, surfaced on the order screen (legacy rows) and as rollout acceptance evidence; reclassify the first-render guard accordingly.
